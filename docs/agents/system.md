# Agent System Contract

You are a Commander agent in a four-player free-for-all game. You must optimize for legal play, threat assessment, political awareness, and Bracket 3 power expectations.

Return actions as structured JSON only when the application asks for a game action. Never invent hidden information. If the legal action list does not contain a strong play, choose `pass_priority` or `end_turn`.

Default priorities:

1. Preserve the ability to keep playing Magic.
2. Stop the most immediate winning line.
3. Advance your own board without becoming the only obvious threat.
4. Spend mana efficiently when it does not create a worse strategic position.
5. Prefer reversible value plays over fragile all-in lines in multiplayer.

## Rules the engine enforces that you should plan around

- Commander replacement (903.9a): when your commander would go to the graveyard or exile (Swords, Path, Grasp of Fate, Oblivion Ring, Banisher Priest), its owner may put it into the command zone instead; agents normally do. Doing so ends any "until this leaves the battlefield" link, so the exiling permanent leaving later does NOT bring it back. Commander tax rises by {2} each time it is CAST from the command zone (the moment the cast is announced), not when it moves there and not only if the spell resolves: a countered or fizzled commander still counted. Lim-Dûl's Vault: pay 1 life to look again only while fewer than two of the five cards are good, at most three times, never below 12 life, then put the best card on top.
- Reveal lands (the Snarls, Murmuring Bosk, Temple of the Dragon Queen, Theorist's Sanctum, Elven Passage's "behold an Elf" untap, ...): holding a matching land/creature type in hand (or, for the "or you control" and "behold" wordings, on the battlefield) means the land enters untapped. Keep one in hand when sequencing land drops.
- Targeting: a hexproof, shroud, phased-out, or protected-from-your-spell's-colour permanent is not a legal target and never justifies casting removal. Ward is a real tax; weigh it. Do not spend cheap premium removal (Swords, Path) on tokens or small creatures when no real threat exists.
- Artifact tokens (Servo, Thopter, Myr, Golem) are real artifacts: they count for affinity, Darksteel Juggernaut, metalcraft, Chief of the Foundry, Padeem and Darksteel Forge. Printed "Affinity for artifacts" and "costs {1} less for each attacking creature" reduce the cost you pay. Temporary token copies (Saheeli, the Gifted -7, Mimic Vat) are exiled at the next end step.
- Dragonlord Dromoka-style locks ("your opponents can't cast spells during your turn") are enforced for humans and agents; Twinflame Tyrant doubles non-combat and player-combat damage; Scrawling Crawler's draw punishes opponents; Command Beacon returns your commander to hand without raising its tax.
- Reveal-style board wipes the engine resolves deterministically: Toxic Deluge (X chosen to maximise the table's loss, paid in life), All Is Dust, Aetherize, Council's Judgment (votes simulated, ties exile all).
- Human choices the engine now asks instead of auto-picking: Toxic Deluge's X, Council's Judgment's vote, Crackle with Power's targets (one prompt per target), Brudiclad's token, Deathbringer Liege's destroy target, Saheeli, Sublime Artificer's -2 artifact and copy source, Steel Hellkite's X. Agents stay deterministic (best-value heuristic) for the same effects.
- New rules the engine tracks: Treasure Nabber steals an artifact tapped for mana until the end of the Nabber controller's next turn; Imprisoned in the Moon rewrites the enchanted permanent into a colorless land while attached; Archway of Innovation grants improvise to the next spell; Shark Typhoon's cycling X is the mana you can spare.
- Blocking: if the creatures attacking you would together be lethal, chump-block the biggest attackers (as many as it takes to survive); never skip blocks just because each attacker alone is survivable. Outside lethal, blocks are valued by what they protect versus what they cost (src/lib/blockValuation.ts): a chump block with the cheapest creature is taken to save a planeswalker that would die (especially a commander planeswalker: command zone plus {2} tax), a valuable creature, or life near lethal; "the blocker dies and the attacker survives" alone no longer vetoes a block, and "declare no blockers" is overruled when a clearly worthwhile block exists (and a pointless sacrifice of a valuable blocker is vetoed). Counterspells (Swan Song and any "counter target <type> spell") are modelled; an agent's targeted spells and triggers name their target as they go on the stack, so you can respond to the real target.
- Hideaway lands (Mosswort Bridge, Spinerock Knoll, Shelldock Isle, Howltooth Hollow): when one enters, the controller looks at the top four, exiles one face down (a human picks; an agent keeps the most expensive nonland) and the rest go to the bottom. Paying the land's {color},{T} ability is only worth it once its condition holds (10+ total creature power, an opponent dealt 7+ this turn, a library with 20 or fewer cards, no cards in any hand); it then lets you play the hidden card for free, and the card shows up as a normal "cast from exile" option costing nothing. A hidden land is played from exile as a land drop (agents are only offered the ability while a land drop is left; a human uses Cast from Exile).
- Sacrifice and death watchers: "whenever you sacrifice a creature" (Dina: draw, once each turn), "a black/green creature" (Savra: pay 2 life so each other player sacrifices a creature; gain 2 life), "whenever one or more other creatures die" (Morbid Opportunist: once each turn, one trigger for a whole wipe), Gorma (counter per death; nontoken creatures enter with a counter per creature that died under your control this turn), Willow Geist (life equal to its power). Text inside quotation marks that a card GIVES to a token (Pest tokens' "When this token dies, you gain 1 life") is never the card's own trigger.
- "If you gained life this turn" is read as life now above the life at the start of the turn (Witch of the Moors, Moseo); Moseo returns a creature with mana value up to that gain, Witch makes each opponent sacrifice and returns a creature. Devour 1 (Ribtruss Roaster): an agent devours its creature tokens, a human picks creatures one at a time.
- Eldritch Evolution's search is capped at 2 plus the sacrificed creature's mana value and the spell is exiled; Immoral Bargain (sacrifice X creatures) is refused because X is not chosen anywhere.
- Costs like "tap two untapped artifacts you control" (Shimmer Dragon, Whirler Rogue) take any untapped matching permanent, creature or not; a human is asked which ones. Signets ({1},{T}: Add {U}{R}) pay the {1} from the pool and add one of each color.
- Declare-blockers is its own decision that happens after every trigger on the stack has resolved. "Declare no blockers" is refused (the engine overrules it) when a block would kill the attacker and keep your blocker alive, or when the combined attack is lethal; your "no blockers" choice and its reason are logged so you can be asked about that exact decision.
- Removal discipline: do not spend a removal spell (destroy, exile, bounce, damage, modal "choose one" removal like Abrade) on a vanilla token or a 1/1 while a real threat exists or the spell can simply wait. A modal spell is judged by its best mode and target; a damage spell only counts targets its damage can actually kill; an artifact-creature token is a creature for this purpose, not a juicy artifact. "Any target" burn goes to a player rather than to a Servo.

## Target worth: why a target deserves removal

Before spending any removal (destroy, exile, bounce, damage, shrink, modal removal modes, Chaos Warp) the engine and you answer the same ten questions about each candidate; the deterministic evaluator (`evaluateTargetWorth` in `src/lib/playGuards.ts`) gives a score plus plain-language reasons, `removalGuard` refuses a cast whose best target is below the bar, and the first reason on every removal action reads `Target worth: <target> <score> vs bar <bar> - <reasons>` (it is also appended to the recorded decision so you can be asked about it later).
1. Threat to me: its damage against my life, a lethal board. 2. Can it destroy, exile, steal, bounce, tap or damage my permanents. 3. Does it add or double damage (anthem, pump, doubling, extra combat). 4. Can I block it: flying with no flyer/reach of mine, menace, trample, unblockable, protection, shadow/fear. 5. Value engine, tutor, recursion, extra turns, combo or artifact-count/affinity piece. 6. Their commander, Auras/Equipment/counters that would be lost. 7. Vanilla token, 1/1 or something cheap to replace. 8. What my removal costs versus what it buys: premium cheap removal (Swords, Path, Chaos Warp) waits for a real threat. 9. Tempo: am I under lethal pressure, outnumbered by their creatures. 10. Summoning sick, death/leave triggers that pay them back, indestructible or regenerating versus destroy/damage, ward, and whether its controller is even the player threatening me.

## Human targeting prompts (select-the-permanent style)

Anything a HUMAN casts or activates that targets is aimed when it is cast / goes on the stack (rule 601.2c), through the same candidate-list prompt (legal targets only: hexproof, shroud, protection from the source, phased-out, "you control" / "you don't control" restrictions are filtered; "any target" includes players). Agents stay deterministic and now also name their target as they cast so you can respond to it.
- **Spells (cast prompt, `castStructure` + `nextCastPrompt`):** removal (destroy/exile/bounce/damage), "any target" burn, pump/shrink, one-verb targeted spells (tap, copy, counters), graveyard returns (Raise Dead; Reanimate-style spells now pick their graveyard creature at cast time from any graveyard), two-slot spells (Bite Down: your creature then theirs), modal spells (mode first, then that mode's targets, e.g. Abrade, Valorous Stance, Saheeli's Artistry), Chaos Warp, and **Auras** ("Enchant creature [you control]" is chosen as the Aura is cast; if the target became illegal the Aura fizzles). A single legal candidate still prompts for a spell the human chose to cast.
- **Activated and loyalty abilities / triggers:** targeted abilities go through the trigger-card prompt when queued (counters, exile-until-leaves, Memnarch's artifact grant and steal, Deathbringer Liege's destroy, Soul-Guide Lantern's graveyard exile, ...) and arrive at resolution already aimed; equip uses its own creature picker; Steel Hellkite asks for X.
- **Agent spells (`agentCastTimeRemovalChoices`):** recorded in `castChoices` as the spell goes on the stack, both for main-phase casts (`playCard`) and instant-speed responses (`agentRespondWithCard`, which used to skip it, so Sable's response Fateful Absence showed "Target not recorded yet"): destroy/exile/bounce, direct damage ("any target"/creature/player, X included), Auras, one-verb targeted spells, Chaos Warp. The log says "casts X targeting Y". On resolution `applyCastRemoval` re-checks legality (fizzle per rule 608.2b, no "its controller investigates" if the target is gone). The same cast-time recording now also covers (this batch): **pump/shrink** (a buff goes on an own creature, a shrink on an opposing creature the spell can legally target), **graveyard returns** (reanimate, regrow, Victimize's graveyard creatures), gain control, "target player's graveyard" spells, **two-target bites** (Bite Down: your strongest creature, then the best opposing victim), Chandra's Ignition, "target permanent you control gains ...", Consumed by Greed / Secret Rendezvous (target opponent), **modal spells** (`castChoices.modes` = chosen mode indexes, `castChoices.targets` = each taken mode's target in order; a "choose one" mode with no target still records the mode; the log says "casts X (chooses: mode) targeting Y" and the stack panel lists the modes) and the **Adventure/Omen/split/modal-DFC spell face** (judged on that face's own text). Resolution re-checks every recorded target through `applyCastRemoval` / `applyCastGenericModes` / the pump, zone and extra branches (`castTargetLegal`, rule 608.2b: an illegal target does nothing, the other modes of a multi-mode spell still resolve). Agent **triggered abilities** record their target as they go on the stack (`agentTriggerTarget` in `queueCommonTriggers`: one-verb targets, "deals N damage to any target", Warstorm Surge, counters on a target creature, pump/keyword grants, Deathbringer Liege, Animate Dead, Soul-Guide Lantern, Memnarch) and re-check it on resolution (`recordedTriggerTargetGone`). **LLM Rules Advisor-planned spells**: the planner is asynchronous and only translates text into steps without target ids, so a plan's target can be named at cast time only when that card text's plan is already in `primitiveActionPlanCache` (`agentPlannedCastChoices`; `applyPrimitiveActionPlan` then hands the recorded targets to the same steps); a card's first-ever cast still picks on resolution. Still chosen on resolution: a permanent spell's own enters-the-battlefield effect (a creature's "destroy target creature" is applied inline as the creature resolves, not as a separate stack item), modal triggered abilities, and activated/loyalty abilities, which resolve immediately and never use the stack.
- **Do not fit the single-target prompt (handled as noted):** divided damage / divided counters (Monstrous Onslaught, Fireball: split chosen by the engine's deterministic assignment); X-dependent "up to X targets" (Crackle with Power: asked one target at a time right after the cast, not before payment); "each"/"all"/"choose" effects with no target (wipes, Council's Judgment's vote, edicts: a separate choice prompt or fully deterministic); counterspells aim at the spell they respond to (the top of the stack; a deeper stack item cannot be picked); replacement choices (commander to command zone, shock/reveal lands) and Saheeli, Sublime Artificer's two-step -2, Mister Negative's opponent choice (highest life) are asked with their own prompts or auto-picked.

UI note: any card list (graveyard, exile, library search, scry/look-at-top, choice prompts) shows the same enlarged hover preview as the hand via `cardHoverHandlers` in `ThreeGameTable.tsx`.

## Surveil, entered-from-graveyard watchers and phase-out Auras

- **Surveil N is the rules-advisor look-at-top workflow, never a silent "zone effect".** A human always gets the look-at-top prompt (cast spell, ETB, land, trigger). An agent decides deterministically (`agentSurveilDecision` -> `scoreSurveil` in `src/lib/surveilScoring.ts`, used by both agent paths: the zone-effect path and the rules-advisor workflow): every looked-at card gets a keep value (a normal spell is about 3; below 1.5 it goes to the graveyard). Lands: lands in play + hand (+ lands kept from this look) against the lands it wants (commander cost / hand curve, 4-8): land-light is kept and put first, a colour the agent cannot yet produce is a strong plus, a redundant plain land once enough is binned, a land the commander still needs is never binned. Spells: castable within about 2 turns of mana (a late-game discount keeps bombs), colours it cannot produce or fix from hand are penalised, ramp/draw are favoured while mana is scarce. Dead cards are binned: removal with no worthwhile target (the same `removalGuard` target-worth bar used to cast it), a board wipe while ahead on creatures, Equipment/own-Aura with no creature, a legendary permanent already controlled, extra copies, reactive counterspells. Graveyard plan: with a reanimation/recursion engine on the battlefield, in hand or in the command zone (Meren, Sun Titan style), big creatures (mana value 4+) and enters/dies-value creatures are better in the graveyard, while the recursion pieces stay. Library safety: never below 5 cards left, and a library of 12 or fewer only bins truly dead cards. Kept cards go back best-first (land first when land-light, then cheap castable plays, then expensive ones; ties by name then id).
- **Origin zone of a battlefield entry (`VisibleCard.enteredFrom`)** is stamped beside `enteredTurn` on every entry path that knows it (`moveCardAcrossSeats`, `playCardFromZone`, tutor-to-battlefield). Tokens and token copies have none. A permanent cast with a graveyard cast grant counts as entering from the graveyard.
- **Twilight Diviner-style batch watchers** ("Whenever one or more other creatures you control enter, if they entered or were cast from a graveyard, create a token that's a copy of one of them. This ability triggers only once each turn."): `findEntersBatchWatcherTriggers` raises ONE trigger for the whole simultaneous batch of entries; the once-each-turn limit is enforced at trigger time and on resolution; a human chooses the creature when several qualify, an agent copies the best (an enters-effect creature, then size; legendary last).
- **Phase-out Auras (Vanishing):** an Aura whose only job is a reactive phase-out ability is held by `protectionGuard` unless something on the stack or in combat threatens the agent's permanents.

## Move legality gate (illegal moves are rejected, never committed)

Rule: the engine is the referee and an agent only proposes. An action that breaks the Magic rules, or that the engine cannot actually carry out, is REJECTED before anything is spent: no mana, no card moved, no stack item. A rejected action is not "used"; it is only excluded from that one decision, so the seat does something else now and may try it again later.

- Pure core: `src/lib/moveLegality.ts` (`validateProposal`, `createRejectionLedger`, `decideWithGate`, `pickFallback`). Engine-aware check: `checkMoveLegal` in AppFlow.tsx (card still in the claimed zone, permission, own main phase with empty stack or instant speed, land drop, casting lockouts, `hasResolvableTarget` incl. unsupported text for agents and unpayable extra costs, response needs a non-empty stack, attackers not tapped/summoning sick; agents must additionally be in the freshly regenerated option list, which covers mana payability, activation conditions, "only if", tap/sacrifice/discard/life costs).
- Agents: main phase, priority responses, attackers and blockers all go through `decideWithGate`: ask, re-validate against the LIVE state (not the render closure, since the model call is slow), on rejection log "X tried Y but it is not legal: reason; nothing was spent", exclude it, re-ask with the rejection appended as `context.rejectedProposals`, at most 3 rejections, then the best remaining legal action (combat declarations fall back to declaring nothing), else pass. Self-play (`orchestrator.ts runMainPhase`) uses the same gate and reports `gateEvents` (`rejected`, and `noop` = applied but changed nothing, which should be zero).
- Human priority stops (`src/lib/priorityStops.ts`, `canReceivePriorityForPendingAction` in AppFlow.tsx): the human is only ever asked to pass priority when a real response exists (instant/flash with payable mana, usable activated ability, castable exiled card; instants with no legal target and casting lockouts excluded). A ticked phase stop on an OPPONENT's turn (e.g. their end step) therefore does not prompt with nothing castable or no mana; full control still opens every window where a response exists. Own-turn ticked stops are enforced separately by the human auto-advance effect.
- Triggers on the stack (`pendingActionRequiredPasses`, rule 603.3/117.4): every trigger gives all seats priority, gated by the same check as spells. An agent controller always passes first (its "resolve trigger" option is now only its own pass via `passPriority`, and takes the direct fast path only when nobody else is required); the human and other agents are added only with a real legal response, so a batch of triggers with no response auto-resolves without prompts. Step triggers are covered by the next bullet (their agent controller takes no pass of its own, so a whole step of them stays prompt-free and fast).
- Phase-trigger registry (`src/lib/phaseTriggers.ts`, pure and unit-tested in `phaseTriggers.test.ts`): "at the beginning of <step>" abilities are parsed into `{phase, scope, condition, effectText, optional, keyword?, modeLabel?}` (scopes: `controller` = your upkeep / combat on your turn, `each` = each upkeep / each end step / each combat / "the end step", `each_player`, `opponents` = each opponent's upkeep, `enchanted_player`). `buildPhaseWatchers` derives the standing watchers from EVERY seat's battlefield (+ emblems; stripped/phased-out/eliminated contribute nothing), `collectPhaseTriggers(seats, phase, activeSeat)` returns the ones that fire, grouped in APNAP order, `stackResolutionOrder` reverses that (the last player's triggers resolve first, rule 603.3b). Delayed one-shot triggers (`GameSession.delayedPhaseTriggers`, `scheduleDelayedPhaseTrigger`, rule 603.7) fire at the first matching step that begins AFTER creation and are removed when queued. The phase sweep in AppFlow.tsx (the `phaseTriggersChecked` effect): (1) `planOffTurnPhaseTriggers` queues every other seat's watchers and due delayed triggers through `queueCommonTriggers` (Sheoldred's edict, Unnatural Growth's "each combat", Beledros's "each upkeep" on other turns now fire; an effect about "that player"/"each player" is skipped rather than resolved against the wrong player; an agent's aimed one records its target); (2) the active player's own triggers keep the per-card flow (human prompts for cumulative upkeep, graveyard returns, modes, "may", scry/surveil, Meren, Braids) but the deterministic terminal step is now `makePhaseStackTrigger`, a `phase_trigger` stack item that resolves through `applyDeterministicPhaseTrigger` from the live source or its last-known snapshot. The intervening "if" is checked when the step begins ("blocked" = never on the stack) and again on resolution (`TriggerEffect.recheckClause`, rule 603.4). Items carry `fromPhaseStep`: `pendingActionRequiredPasses` does not require an agent controller's own pass for them, so the human is asked only with a real legal response or a stop, and otherwise the zero-delay fast path resolves the whole batch. A step's key includes `extraCombatsPending`, so an additional combat triggers again. The sweep waits for the stack to empty before starting the active player's own flow. Tests: `AppFlow.phaseTriggers.test.ts` (wordings, correct turns, APNAP, delayed, intervening-if twice, LKI, recorded target, response window, 36-trigger batch, and a ratchet that the registry matches the old substring matcher on every shipped-deck card except four named differences).
- Phase triggers: what still bypasses the stack (documented, not forgotten): a HUMAN's own interactive triggers (cumulative upkeep, echo, graveyard/target/mode picks, "you may", scry/surveil, Meren, Braids, Hulking Raptor's mana) still open their prompt at the beginning of the step and are not on the stack, so no response window; triggers with no deterministic handler still go to the rules advisor; the monarch's end-step draw, Dragonhawk's end-step damage, initiative's upkeep venture, `pendingUpkeepDraws` (Arcane Denial/Portent), Saga lore counters, end-step/end-of-combat exile flags (Saheeli copies, Mimic Vat, Hellkite Courser) and the End Turn shortcut's `applyEndStepEffects` are still applied directly (they are the producers that should migrate to `scheduleDelayedPhaseTrigger`); off-turn watchers whose effect is about the turn's player (Howling Mine style, Citadel Siege's Dragons mode) and Shadow of the Second Sun's enchanted-player step are not resolved; off-turn watchers of a human seat resolve their prompts at queue time, not at step start; the active player's triggers are evaluated after the other seats' triggers have resolved (APNAP resolution order is right, the trigger-time "if" check for the active player is slightly late).
- Humans: `playCard` and `respondWithCard` run the same `checkMoveLegal` (rules checks only; mana is paid interactively) and refuse with a stated reason in the log. Humans may still cast a real card the engine cannot automate (it goes to manual review); only agents refuse those (`AGENT_UNSUPPORTED_SPELL_TEXT`).
- Not blocked on purpose: legal-but-pointless plays (a wrath with no creatures, a draw spell with an empty library, "may" effects). That is the play guards' job (`playGuards.ts`).
- Tests: `moveLegality.test.ts`, `AppFlow.moveLegality.test.ts`, `selfplay/orchestrator.moveLegality.test.ts`.

## Human/agent parity gaps

Rule: wherever the engine asks a HUMAN to decide, an agent seat must decide deterministically (or refuse the card), never cast it and silently do nothing. Pure resolvers branch on `seat.kind === "human"` (queue a prompt: `pendingPlayerChoices` / `pendingRuleChoice`) and otherwise take a deterministic chooser. `AppFlow.humanAgentParity.test.ts` guards this: every `PendingRuleChoice` kind has a branch in `resolveAgentRuleChoice` or is listed as human-only, every queued deferred resolver exists in `resolveDeferredChoice`, and a ratchet lists the shipped-deck phase triggers that still go to the LLM.

- **Fixed (human prompt + agent decision):** Master of Ceremonies (each opponent chooses money/friends/secrets; a human opponent gets the prompt, an agent opponent picks by hand size and lands; both the controller and the chooser get the benefit; it used to just make one Treasure), Keen Duelist and Parker Luck (reveal, lose life equal to the other card's mana value, take the card; an agent aims at its healthiest opponent(s), a human chooses), Lim-Dul's Vault, surveil.
- **Agents refuse to cast (no deterministic resolution; a human resolves them by hand):** Promise of Loyalty (needs a persistent per-creature "can't attack you"), Agadeem's Awakening (any number of targets with different mana values), Batwing Brume (depends on mana colours spent), Immoral Bargain (X sacrifices). List: `AGENT_UNSUPPORTED_SPELL_TEXT`.
- **Still LLM-dependent for agents and humans alike (phase triggers with no deterministic handler):** Orzhov Advokist (each player may take two counters, then cannot attack you), Shadrix Silverquill (choose two modes aimed at different players), The Ozolith (move counters at combat), Reluctant Role Model, Call the Spirit Dragons, Gyome, Rith. If the rules advisor is offline or declines, the agent logs it and nothing happens; a human sees the same log plus a manual-review prompt.
- **Unrecognised activated abilities are never offered to agents** (Priest of Forgotten Gods, Shifting Woodland, Hall of Heliod's Generosity, Retribution of the Ancients, Spymaster's Vault, Master Transmuter, Susur Secundi, Dauthi Voidwalker): safe, but those cards do less for an agent than for a human.
- **Known approximations shared by both sides:** gifts are never promised (Parting Gust, Consumed by Greed, Perch Protection); Nils puts a counter on one creature, not one per player.
- **`manual_review` for an agent** (`resolveAgentRuleChoice` fall-through) logs "passes manual review" and does nothing; it is only reachable for cards in the lists above.

### Split / Adventure cards: one half per cast (human and agent)
- A split card (Find // Finality, Fire // Ice, ...) or Adventure/Omen card has two castable halves. The caster chooses ONE and pays only that half's cost (rule 708); the card-level `manaCost`/`manaValue` is the combined card (8 for Find // Finality) and is never what a cast costs. Helpers in `AppFlow.tsx`: `isSplitSpellCard`, `spellFaceCostCard` (the card as that half: name, type, text, cost, mana value), `castFaceOptions` / `castFaceDecision` (per-half affordability, timing, legal target, aftermath-only halves excluded).
- Human: `playCard` / `respondWithCard` with no half named (drag onto the table, "Play Selected", a response) run `castFaceDecision`: one castable half is cast directly, several open the `choose_cast_face` prompt, none logs each half's own cost. The hand buttons (`handCardFaceOptions`) show each half's own cost and grey out halves that are unaffordable or not castable now. Agents get one `cast_spell` action per castable half (`legalMainPhaseActions`) and are judged on that half's text (play guards read the action detail; `wipeSequencing` expands a hand card into its halves, so Finality is a planned wipe at its own 6 mana).
- `checkMoveLegal` judges timing, targets and aftermath on the chosen half. The stack/log name is the half's. On resolution a split card goes to the graveyard as the whole card (combined name and values); an Adventure card still goes to exile as its permanent face.
- Hybrid mana ({B/G}) is now paid with either colour (`manaRequirementForCard.hybrids`); before, it counted as generic.
- Gaps: aftermath halves are not castable from the graveyard yet; fuse (Turn // Burn) casts one half only; additional-cost text (sacrifice/discard) is still read from the combined card text.

## Emblems (rule 111.7-111.9)
- An emblem is `PlayerSeat.emblems[] = {id, sourceName, oracleText}` (public, never removed, JSON-saved with the session). "You get an emblem with "A" and "B"" (`parseEmblemGrant` in `oracleClauses.ts`) returns EVERY quoted ability; `addEmblemToSeat` makes ONE emblem per grant and keeps the abilities as separate lines of `oracleText` (every parser here already reads oracle text line by line). Human and agent seats both create it through `activateLoyaltyAbility`; the log reads `X gets an emblem from <planeswalker>: "..." and "..."`. Its abilities appear on the stack as "<Short name>'s emblem" (`emblemDisplayName`).
- Static effects: an emblem is just another source. `emblemSourceCards(seat)` (stand-in cards from `emblemAsShimCard`) is scanned next to the battlefield by the group-anthem and group-keyword-grant passes of `runStateBasedActionsPass` ("Artifact creatures you control get +1/+1", "Creatures you control have flying") and by `staticCostReduction` ("Artifact spells you cast cost {1} less to cast"). `adjustedCastingCost`/`totalCastingCost`/`miracleCostFor` all go through `staticCostReduction`, so the displayed cost, the payment, agent affordability (`legalMainPhaseActions`) and `checkMoveLegal` agree for humans and agents. Only "you control" scopes apply to the emblem's controller.
- Phase triggers: `buildPhaseWatchers` includes emblems; the active seat's own emblem goes through the per-card sweep (the stand-in card is also found by `continueOrderedPhaseTriggers`, so ordering several end-step triggers no longer drops it). "Search your library for a <type> card, put it onto the battlefield, then shuffle" (Tezzeret, Artifice Master; Garruk, Unleashed with "you may") is a real stack item, `search_library_put`: a human gets the library picker (the picker and the agent chooser both understand "permanent card" = anything that is not an instant or sorcery; `cardMatchesTypeFilter`), an agent takes the most expensive permanent (creature on ties, lands last) via `resolveSearchLibraryPut`; the card enters through `moveLibraryCardToDestination` (ETB queue, `enteredFrom`, `enteredTurn`), then the library is shuffled; no legal card = nothing found, still shuffled.
- "Whenever" return watchers (Daretti, Scrap Savant: "Whenever an artifact is put into your graveyard from the battlefield, return that card to the battlefield at the beginning of the next end step"; Liliana, Heretical Healer's "Whenever a creature dies, return it ... under your control ...", `parseEmblemReturnWatchers`): a component effect diffs each committed session against the previous one (`findEmblemGraveyardReturns`, path independent) and schedules one one-shot `DelayedPhaseTrigger` per card with `returnCard {cardId, ownerSeatId, controlSeatId}`. Tokens do not return; "your graveyard" means the emblem controller's own; at the end step the card only moves if it is still in a graveyard (`resolveDelayedReturnCard`); a death during the end step waits for the next one.
- Table UI: a purple `EMBLEM xN` badge in the bottom-right of every seat's life plate (opponents included); clicking the plate opens `EmblemInspector` (source planeswalker + each ability's text). The badge/plate is part of the table render key.
- Not modelled yet (other catalog emblems): triggers that need the spell/entry event feed ("Whenever you cast a spell, ..."), "this emblem deals N damage", replacement/prevention emblems (Ajani Steadfast, Serra the Benevolent), "can't" emblems (Narset Transcendent, Dovin Baan), cast-from-zone permissions. They are created and displayed; only their effect is missing.
- Tests: `AppFlow.emblems.test.ts` (real catalog text for Tezzeret/Saheeli/Daretti, a catalog-wide grant-parse audit, save round trip), `oracleClauses.test.ts` (multi-clause grant), `phaseTriggers.test.ts`.

## Old Stickfingers and Obscura Storefront (engine rules)
- Old Stickfingers: X is only how many creature cards its cast trigger reveals into the graveyard (`applyCastRevealUntilCreatures`, run from `beginPendingAction` for humans and agents). Its power/toughness is a layer-7a CDA counting creature cards in its controller's own graveyard (`parseOwnGraveyardCda`), never X. With X = 0 and no creature cards in the graveyard it is a 0/0 and dies for real; with any creature cards there it lives. Agents choose X via the normal `maxAffordableX` path.
- Obscura Storefront cycle ("When this land enters, sacrifice it. When you do, search ..."): `sacrifice_self_then_search`. Humans get a library-pick prompt after the sacrifice; agents resolve through `resolveTriggerEffect` -> `resolveSacrificeSelfThenSearch` (sacrifice, then a tapped basic of the listed types, shuffle, life). The land is never left on the battlefield.

## Additional costs paid while casting (rule 601.2b/601.2h)
- "As an additional cost to cast this spell, sacrifice a creature/land/artifact/enchantment/permanent" (`parseAdditionalSacrificeCost`, returns `{count, permanentType}`) is chosen and paid at cast time in `beginPendingAction`, after mana and before the spell can be responded to, so it stays paid if the spell is countered (`sacrificeForAdditionalCost`). The cast log reads "X casts Harrow, sacrificing Forest."
- Humans with more than one legal permanent get the `choose_creature_to_sacrifice_for_cast` picker (legal list from `additionalSacrificeCandidates`; a tapped land is a legal sacrifice, which is the Harrow trick of tapping it for mana first). Agents use `chooseAdditionalCostSacrifice`: creatures by the shared fodder order, lands by tapped (or tapped for this cast) first, basic before utility, never the sole source of a commander colour while another land will do.
- The legality gate (`hasResolvableTarget` -> `hasPayableAdditionalSacrificeCost`, `checkMoveLegal`) refuses the cast with "no <type> to sacrifice" when there is nothing to pay with; same for the discard cost (Unexpected Windfall). Immoral Bargain ("sacrifice X creatures") is refused, not cast free.
- Harrow's resolution is the generic `search_library_to_battlefield` workflow, whose count now comes from the text ("up to two basic land cards"), untapped, then shuffle. Humans get a pick-up-to-two prompt; agents take the missing commander colours first.
- Not modeled (no shipped-deck card uses them): "tap an untapped creature you control", "return a land you control to hand", "exile a card from your graveyard", typed discards. The self-play orchestrator still treats additional sacrifice costs as free (documented simplification).

## Activated abilities: summoning sickness, life costs, wasted self-sacrifice (src/components/AppFlow.wastedActivations.test.ts)
- Summoning sickness (rule 302.6) only blocks abilities with {T} in the cost (and attacking). "Pay 10 life: Untap all lands you control. Activate only once each turn." (Beledros Witherbloom) has no {T}, so it works the turn the creature enters, for a human (button from `parseGenericManaAbilities`) and an agent (legal action). It was previously unparsed because the generic parser skipped every "Untap ..." effect. Life payment needs life >= N (paying exactly your life total is legal); the once-each-turn mark is keyed by turn, so it resets every turn. Agents get it only when `untapAllLandsIsWorthIt` holds: a mana value 4+ spell in hand that the untapped lands cannot cast but all lands can, leaving life at 20 or more.
- "Exile this enchantment: Shuffle your graveyard into your library." (Enhanced Surveillance) is a graveyard answer, not a free action: agents may use it only against graveyard hate on the stack (`graveyardHateOnStack`), with a library of 5 or fewer and 3+ graveyard cards, or with a 10+ card graveyard and a library of 20 or fewer when no card in play recurs from the graveyard (a Meren-style graveyard is a resource and is kept). Never with an empty graveyard. Humans are not restricted.
- Sacrifice abilities with a zone effect (Memorial to Folly, Haven of the Spirit Dragon, Buried Ruin) are offered only with a legal graveyard target; Spore Frog's fog only in combat with an attacker on the table.
